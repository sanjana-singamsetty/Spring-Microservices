Definition and Role in Online Transactions
A payment gateway is a technology that securely transmits online payment data from a customer to a merchant’s acquiring bank. It acts as a bridge between the payment processor and the merchant, encrypting sensitive card information and ensuring that transactions are approved or declined in real-time. Without a payment gateway, digital businesses would be unable to accept payments online or manage them securely.

The process starts when a customer submits their payment details on a checkout page. The payment gateway encrypts this data and transmits it to the payment processor. The processor then contacts the cardholder’s bank for authorization. Once approved, the gateway notifies the merchant, and the transaction is completed — all within seconds. Secure Socket Layer (SSL) encryption and tokenization are standard methods used to ensure data protection.

Hosted Gateways redirect users to a third-party page (e.g., PayPal) to complete the transaction. These are easier to implement and typically come with built-in compliance features.
Integrated Gateways allow users to complete the transaction directly on the merchant’s website or app. These API-based solutions offer better user experiences but require robust development and compliance handling.

Popular Providers and Their Differences
Some leading providers include Stripe, PayPal, Razorpay, Authorize.Net, and Square. While Stripe is favored for developer-friendly APIs, PayPal stands out for its global reach and ease of use. Razorpay is popular in the Indian market due to its localized offerings and affordability.

How to Choose the Right Payment Gateway for Your Business
Key Features to Consider
When selecting a gateway, businesses should evaluate:

Supported Payment Methods and Currencies: Ensure it supports credit cards, digital wallets, and multi-currency transactions.
Security Standards: Look for PCI DSS compliance, tokenization, and fraud detection mechanisms.
Extra Features: Recurring billing, refund handling, and chargeback management are critical for many business models.
